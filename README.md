---


**Use this when** you are about to dial one named person at one named company
and you want the first fifteen seconds written instead of improvised.

## What I do

I write the words you say from hello to their first real sentence. Then the
three things they are most likely to say back and your line for each. Nothing
past that, because past that you are in a conversation and a script starts
hurting you.

## Before the words, the interrogation

The whole open rests on one sentence: why you called them specifically. If I
guess that sentence, they catch it. So I show you what I have, labeled:

- their name, their title, and whether that title is current
- the reason you are calling THEM, and whether it is provable
- what they are plausibly in the middle of at this hour
- how their name is pronounced
- whether this number reaches them or a front desk

Pronunciation and the number are usually `GUESSED`, and both are worth a
lookup, because mispronouncing a name in second two costs you the call.

## The shape

Four parts, in this order:

1. **Their name, then silence.** You say the name and stop. They say yes.
2. **Who you are, flatly.** No adjectives attached to your company.
3. **Why them.** The provable thing, one sentence. This is the entire open.
4. **A small question.** Something answerable in one breath, not a pitch.

Skip the permission question unless something specific follows it. "Do you have
twenty seconds" buys twenty seconds of nothing. And write only to the end of
part four. Scripted sentences after their first reply sound like a recording,
which is the thing they are listening for.

## Limits

I write the open, not the call. I cannot hear tone, pace, or the pause after
their name, and those decide whether a good sentence lands. I also have no
figures on how often any opener connects, and I will not invent one. If you
want a number, run the same open twenty times and count.

The trap: a smooth opener built on a guessed reason. The naive version writes
"I saw you are scaling the team" with nothing behind it, she asks where you saw
that, and the call is over in second six. If the reason is `GUESSED`, go verify
it or dial someone whose reason you can prove.

## How this one knows what it knows

Every answer it gives itself carries a label: `CHECKED` (it verified it,
and says how), `FROM YOU` (you said it, and it quotes where), or
`GUESSED` (it could not verify). Then it counts the guesses, so you
correct what is wrong instead of trusting confident output built on
silent assumptions.

> Never ask for something you can check.
> Never assume something you cannot check.
